About Me

Healthcare data that benefits society, not just spreadsheets

I'm Andre Weissmann, a data analyst with deep roots in healthcare operations and a genuine obsession with health data and how it moves through healthcare systems. When data is analyzed with care, it does more than inform decisions. It improves lives.

My path into analytics started with curiosity and grew through real work. Working inside healthcare billing operations at Andres Medical Billing / EMS Management & Consultants gave me domain knowledge that most analysts don't have: I understand the data before it reaches the dashboard.

I've built projects across SQL, Python, Power BI, Tableau, and Excel. Each one started with a real question, not a tutorial prompt. Working inside healthcare operations taught me something most analysts learn late: the messiest part of data isn't the cleaning. It's knowing what to ask of it.

My passion with data grows every day.

Nashville Housing: Turning 56,477 Corrupt Records Into a Trusted Data Asset

A real estate dataset with missing addresses, duplicated parcels, and four inconsistent values where two should exist cannot support any downstream decision. Using SQL Server, I applied seven data cleaning methods: self-join imputation for missing addresses, PARSENAME to split owner and property address columns, CASE WHEN standardization across 56,477 rows, CTE-plus-ROW_NUMBER deduplication, and date format normalization. The output is a fully queryable, trustworthy table any analyst or business team can build on without second-guessing the data.

Data Professionals Survey: Does Education Level Actually Drive Salary?

              
630 data professionals across the US, India, Canada, and UK reported their salaries, tools, and satisfaction. The central question was whether education level predicts compensation. The Power BI dashboard I built across five charts and two KPI cards answers it clearly: PhD holders average $206K, yet bachelor's degree data scientists average $93K and outperform associates and masters tiers in total employment volume. Education level alone is not the salary driver people assume. The real signal is role, not credential. That distinction matters to every hiring manager setting compensation bands and every early-career analyst deciding whether to go back to school.

Seattle Airbnb 2016: The Three Variables That Explain Every Pricing Decision

              
323,346 booking records across Seattle's Airbnb market. Seven missing zip codes manually corrected through neighborhood cross-referencing. An inner join across Listings and Calendar tables to connect property attributes to revenue timing. What emerged were three clean levers any host can act on: zip code 98134 commands the city's highest average nightly rate, a four-bedroom generates nearly four times the revenue of a one-bedroom, and the week of December 25th ($2,110,350 in city-wide revenue) outperforms the week of March 27th ($1,906,735). Gut-feel pricing ignores all three. This Tableau dashboard makes all three visible and interactive.

BMI and Waist-to-Hip Ratio: Clinical Risk Screening in Plain Language

              
Raw BMI scores are screening tools, not diagnoses. This Python program applies the CDC formula across four clinical weight categories, then optionally calculates Waist-to-Hip Ratio using WHO thresholds (0.90 for men, 0.85 for women) to add a cardiovascular risk dimension BMI alone cannot provide. The output is not a number to Google. It is a plain-English assessment: your classification, your risk tier, and what it means, delivered in seconds to a patient or provider with no medical background required.

Bike Sales: Pacific Region Shows Strong Margins — On a Smaller Sample Worth Watching

              
13,351 sales records across Europe, North America, and the Pacific region. After deduplication and field standardization (marital status, gender, and age brackets via nested IF formula), four pivot tables and an interactive slicer dashboard revealed one clear finding: the Pacific region generates the highest profit margins in bike sales. The highest-income buyer profile is a married Pacific-region homeowner in a management role averaging $90,000 annually. The farthest commute distances are also in the Pacific region, where middle-aged female buyers ride more than 10 miles. For any stakeholder allocating sales or marketing investment, this dataset points to one region, one demographic, and one clear next move.
